DIY EEG sessions

The trend is one measurement across days. Open one run to see its waveform or spectrum. Traces are median-centered and drawn at 40 samples per second. The saved recording is still the full stream.

What the words mean

Eyes closed is the 8–12 Hz rhythm with the eyes shut, divided by the same rhythm with the eyes open. O1, Oz, and O2 are the back of the head. A result above 1 is the Berger effect: that rhythm grew when the eyes shut.

Fz focus is the 4–8 Hz rhythm at the front of the head during serial 7, divided by the eyes-open stare. Serial 7 is the minute of silent subtraction. Above 1 means that slower rhythm grew while calculating. A day without serial 7 has no focus point.

Engagement index is beta (13–30 Hz) divided by alpha (8–12 Hz) plus theta (4–8 Hz), at Fz. Theta/beta is theta divided by beta. The session line gives both for the eyes-open stare and for serial 7. The trend buttons show the serial-7 level. On this task a lower engagement index and a higher theta/beta during serial 7 usually mean the slow frontal rhythm grew, which is the same change as Fz focus. They are not a separate focus grade.

On the ratio charts, the dashed line is 1, where the two sides of the ratio are equal. Engagement and theta/beta are levels, so that line is left off those charts. A run under 30 seconds is marked as short because a 20-second block is a weaker day-to-day comparison than a 60-second one.

The wall supply leaks a steady tone into the electrodes. That tone is 50 Hz in some countries and 60 Hz in others, and it would otherwise show up inside the scores. Scores and these drawings remove a narrow notch at the frequency saved for that sitting. A sitting with no location uses 50 Hz. The saved recording is left untouched.

    What this chart is showing

    Each point is one saved run. The buttons choose which measurement is drawn. O1, Oz, O2, and Fz focus are ratios. Fz engagement and Fz theta/beta are the serial-7 levels; the stare level for that run is written under the session. Unchecking the short runs hides blocks under 30 seconds, so the 60-second days can be read on their own. A failed quality check stays off this chart and remains in the session list.

    On the spectrum, color is the channel. A solid line is eyes open, a dashed line is eyes closed, and a dotted line is serial 7.

    What this view is showing

    Waveform. Each checked channel is its own line, with the large offset removed so the rhythm sits on a center line. The shaded blocks behind it are the parts of the sitting: settle, eyes open, eyes closed, and serial 7 when that run has it. The drawing uses 40 samples a second so the rhythm is visible. The file on disk is still the full 500 Hz recording. A channel that never moved is left off the list.

    Spectrum. This is how much of each speed of rhythm was present, from 2 to 30 Hz. The shaded regions are theta at 4–8 Hz, alpha at 8–12 Hz, and beta at 13–30 Hz. Color is the channel. A solid line is eyes open, a dashed line is eyes closed, and a dotted line is serial 7. A higher line inside a shaded region means more of that rhythm.
